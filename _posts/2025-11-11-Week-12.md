### Studying:

Initially, I familiarised myself with the different components. I’ve seen resistors, GND, GPIO and 3v3 before, but I'm unfamiliar with the rest. After doing some research, I found out that J1 is a Sleeve Ring Tip connector. This type of connector allows multiple positive leads in one connection. The parts with a “D” label are Schottky diodes. They allow current to flow in one direction, only if the voltage on the anode (+) is higher than their cathode (-). 
After understanding the individual parts, it was time to put them together. The piezo is connected to a 100kΩ resistor serving as a circuit protector. Considering the resistance of the GPIO pin, the current will flow to the next part of the circuit. In that part, D1, R9, D9 and R17 serve as a voltage clamp, binding it between 0V and 3.3V. This is important because striking a piezo can cause an oscillation between 20V and -20V, which is far too high for a GPIO pin; furthermore, the shape of the signal needs to be conserved while scaling it down. Considering this, the author set a reference voltage between 0 and 3.3V. That way, the oscillation of the piezo is scaled down between 0V and 3.3V; furthermore, they set the diodes in such a way that excess voltage dissipates, and prevents reverse leakage current.
